# Discovery Call Guide — Internal
### Black Car App · 60–75 minutes · Blueprint AI Consulting Co.

**Do not send this to the client.** This is your run sheet. The 10-question form softens him up; this is where you actually get what you need to quote.

**Goal of the call:** leave with enough pricing detail to build a rules engine, a decision on how money moves, and a clear picture of whether the driver network is real.

---

## Open (5 min)

Don't start with questions. Start with the prototype. Open the laptop, walk the three screens, let him react.

> "Before I ask you anything — let me show you what I've been thinking."

People react to pixels. He'll tell you more in two minutes of reacting to a screen than in twenty minutes of you asking about his workflow.

---

## Block 1 — Pricing (30 min · the whole reason for this call)

Get the three invoices on the table. For each one, work backward from the total.

**The script:**
> "Walk me through this one. Where did the $340 come from?"

Then keep pulling. Every time he gives you a number, ask what would change it.

**What you must leave with — check them off as you get them:**

☐ Base rate structure per vehicle class (hourly? per-mile? zone? flat?)
☐ Hourly minimums — and whether they change by day of week or season
☐ Airport flats: which airports, which zones, arrival vs. departure difference
☐ Gratuity: auto-added? what %? can the client change it? does the driver get all of it?
☐ Wait time: free grace period (regular vs. airport), rate after, billing increment
☐ Fuel surcharge — flat, %, or none
☐ Tolls and parking — passed through or baked in
☐ Meet & greet fee
☐ Extra stop fee
☐ After-hours definition and surcharge
☐ Holiday list and surcharge
☐ Child seat fee
☐ Out-of-area / deadhead charge
☐ Cancellation windows and penalties (differ by vehicle class?)
☐ Cleaning / damage fee
☐ Which clients have negotiated rates, and how many

**Probe questions that surface hidden rules:**
- "What if they'd kept you an extra 40 minutes?"
- "What if this had been a Saturday? New Year's Eve?"
- "What if the flight landed two hours late?"
- "What if they added a stop on the way?"
- "Would you have charged your best client the same thing?"
- "When was the last time you gave someone a break on price? Why?"

**Red flag to listen for:** "it depends" or "I just kind of know." That means the rule is in his head, not written down — which means it'll surface as a change order mid-build. Push until it's a rule you could write in code. If it genuinely can't be systematized, note it as a manual-override case and build the override.

---

## Block 2 — Money (10 min)

☐ Card processor and rate
☐ When the card gets charged (booking / after / invoice)
☐ Corporate accounts: how many, what % of revenue, what terms
☐ How drivers get paid — payroll, per ride, weekly settlement
☐ Does he want the app paying drivers, or does that stay manual for now
☐ Average ticket
☐ Annual revenue ballpark

> **Why revenue matters to you specifically:** this is the number your rev-share gets calculated against. Get it now, casually, before the deal conversation. If he won't answer, that itself is information.

---

## Block 3 — The Network (10 min)

This determines whether Phase 2 is a real business or a fantasy.

☐ Individual drivers or partner companies?
☐ Any written agreements today, or handshake?
☐ How does he currently reach them — group text? phone tree? Facebook group?
☐ What does he pay them, and what's his margin on a farmed-out job?
☐ Does he verify insurance and licensing? How?
☐ Would they actually use an app? Has he ever tried to get them on one?
☐ How many could he get on the platform in 30 days if he pushed?

**The honest read:** if "hundreds of drivers" turns out to be a Facebook group he posts in occasionally, Phase 2 is a much longer road and your rev-share math changes. Better to know now.

---

## Block 4 — Clients and Vision (10 min)

☐ Revenue mix by segment
☐ Do top clients book directly, or through an assistant?
☐ Cities and airports served
☐ Any airport permits or staging restrictions
☐ Who does he lose business to, and why
☐ What he charges vs. Uber Black in his market
☐ **The preference question** — what's the most specific thing a client asked him to remember? *(Ask this one even if he answered it on the form. The verbal answer is always better, and it usually opens up three more.)*
☐ What does success look like in 12 months, in dollars

---

## Block 5 — Close (5 min)

Do not leave without:

1. **Naming the phasing as his decision.** "Sounds like the right first move is your fleet and your clients, and the network piece comes after. Agreed?"
2. **A date for the fixed quote.** "I'll have scope and a fixed number to you by Friday."
3. **The rev-share conversation opened** — not closed, just opened. "I'd rather take less cash and a piece of what this does. Want me to put both structures in front of you?"
4. **The assets list.** Photos, logo, rate sheet. Nothing gets built without real photos.

---

## After the call — same day, while it's fresh

- Write the pricing rules into a structured spec. If you can't express a rule as a formula, you don't have it yet — go back and ask.
- Flag every "it depends" as a risk item.
- Draft scope + out-of-scope list.
- Send a recap email with the pricing rules written out and a line that says *"if anything here is wrong, tell me now."* That email is your scope protection later.

---

## Things NOT to say on this call

- ❌ "That's easy" / "That'll take me like a day" — kills your pricing power permanently
- ❌ Any estimate of build hours
- ❌ Agreeing to a feature on the spot. Always: *"Let me scope that and get back to you."*
- ❌ Discussing the rev-share percentage before you know his revenue
